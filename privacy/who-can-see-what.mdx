---
title: "Who can see what"
description: "How access works, in plain English — and what is recorded when someone reads a record."
---

Counselees tell their counselor things they have told no one else. Everything below follows from taking that seriously.

## Counselors see their own counselees

Your caseload is what you can see. Another counselor's counselees aren't hidden behind a permission you could ask for — they're simply not there. Search doesn't find them, the command palette doesn't offer them, and a link to one of their records answers exactly as a link to a record that doesn't exist.

That boundary holds **inside** shared work too. If two counselors are working with a couple, each sees their own notes on their own person. Sharing a case doesn't pool the record.

## What a case grant does and doesn't open

When someone is given access to a case — for supervision, for a handover, for a second opinion — they get the **case record and the supervision entries on it**. That's the whole list.

They do **not** get notes, files, sessions, measurements, homework, care plans, messages, prayer requests or email history. Those stay with the person who wrote them.

## Administrators, and the reason they have to give

A practice administrator runs the practice: the team, billing, booking hours, templates, reports. Running a practice doesn't require reading its counseling.

So when an administrator opens clinical content, the app asks **why** before showing it. There's no way past that box, and what they type is written into the record of the read alongside their name and the time. It's a door that can be opened when it genuinely needs to be — a counselor is ill, a complaint is being investigated — and one that leaves a mark every time.

Some things an administrator cannot do at all, reason or no reason: they can't write in a counselor's clinical record. That's refused outright.

## Intake staff

Intake staff handle enquiries, scheduling and paperwork coming in the door. They don't see counseling content — not with a reason, not on request. Their screens are the intake queue, the calendar and the forms.

## Counselees

A counselee signs in to **My care** and sees their own appointments, homework, documents and messages — nothing about anyone else, including other members of their own family who are counseled at the same practice.

## Reads are recorded, not just changes

Most systems log what was changed. This one also logs what was **read**: who opened a clinical record, when, and — for an administrator — why. Practice administrators can read that log from the **Audit log** card in Practice.

## Nothing sensitive goes out by email

Emails from Soul Care Manager tell you something is waiting. They never carry the thing itself: no session notes, no homework content, no form answers, no attachments. To read it, you sign in.

The same rule covers the technical records the system keeps for itself — they identify records by number, never by name or content.

## Records are stored encrypted

Clinical text and uploaded files are encrypted where they're stored, with each practice holding its own key. Files are handed to you only after you've been checked — a file's address is never a link that works for whoever happens to have it.

## Nothing clinical is deleted

Signed notes and sessions can't be edited or removed; corrections are added to them and both versions stay. Counselees are discharged or archived rather than deleted. Assignments and rosters close and reopen rather than being overwritten, so the history reads correctly years later.
