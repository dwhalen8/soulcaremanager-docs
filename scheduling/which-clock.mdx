---
title: "Which clock is which"
description: "Why a planning screen and a session record can show different times, and which one to trust for what."
---

Soul Care Manager shows times on two different clocks on purpose. Knowing which is which takes a minute and saves a lot of confusion if you travel, or if your practice has counselors in more than one place.

## The two clocks

| Clock | Where it's used |
|---|---|
| **Your clock** — the timezone of wherever you are | **Today** and **Schedule**: the screens you use to plan your day |
| **The practice's clock** — the timezone set on the practice | The counseling record itself: sessions, and corrections to them |

Planning screens follow you because a question like "what time do I need to be ready?" is about where *you* are. The record stays put because a session time is billed against, corrected later by permanent addendum, and read years afterwards by someone who wasn't in the room — and that time has to mean one thing forever.

## So the two can disagree

If your practice is in Denver and you're in Chicago for a week, a Tuesday session can read **3:00 pm** on your Schedule and **2:00 pm** on the session record. Both are right. They're the same moment described from two places.

**Every screen that shows a session time says which clock it's using**, so you never have to guess. On a session record it reads like this:

> Times shown in the practice's timezone (America/Denver); yours is UTC

## Which one to use

- Planning your day, getting to an appointment on time → **your clock**, on Today or Schedule.
- Anything about the record — what time a session started, correcting a time, reading a locked session → **the practice's clock**, as shown on the record.
- Typing a correction → you're typing a practice-clock time. The session record's own time is the one to compare against, not your Schedule.

## Things that repeat

A repeating appointment, your booking hours and your time off are all stored as a rule rather than as a list of moments — "every Tuesday at 2:00 pm", not "these forty specific times". This is what makes them survive a daylight-saving change: a 2:00 pm session stays at 2:00 pm in March, instead of drifting to 1:00 or 3:00 the way a fixed list would.
