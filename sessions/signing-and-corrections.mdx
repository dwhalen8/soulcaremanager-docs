---
title: "Signing, locking and corrections"
description: "What signing does, why it can't be undone, and how to correct something afterwards."
---

A counseling record is read years after it's written, sometimes by people who weren't there. Soul Care Manager treats it that way: you finish a note or a session, you sign it, and from that moment it stops changing. Anything that needs to be put right is **added**, never swapped in.

## Drafts

Before you sign, a note is a draft and behaves like an ordinary document. It saves as you type, you can rewrite it as often as you like, and you can throw it away entirely with **Discard draft**. A draft is the one thing in the app that can actually be deleted.

## Signing a note

When the note says what you want it to say, click **Sign**. Unsigned notes wait on **Today** until you do. See [Writing notes](/sessions/writing-notes).

<Warning>
  Signing locks the note permanently. It can't be edited, unsigned or deleted afterwards.
</Warning>

That's the point of it. A record that could be quietly revised later isn't evidence of anything.

## Completing a session

A session works the same way. While it's running you have **Stop timer**, **Adjust times** and **Complete session**. When you're finished, click **Complete session**; the app asks you to confirm with **Complete & lock**, and after that the session is part of the permanent record.

Once it's locked, the writing boxes are gone from the screen rather than grayed out: a locked record doesn't offer you boxes you can't type in. It reads *Complete · locked*.

## Correcting something afterwards

Locked doesn't mean stuck with a mistake. A locked session or a signed note offers **Add addendum**. On a session, the composer asks which of two you're filing:

| Kind | Use it when |
|---|---|
| **Addendum — add information** | You have something to add: a detail remembered afterwards, a development between sessions, a clarification |
| **Correction — the record was wrong** | Something recorded is wrong: the start time, the end time, or a score you entered |

Either way, the original stays exactly as it was and your addition is stamped with your name and the time. The record then shows both: a corrected time reads as the new time with **corrected by addendum** beside it, and the original value is still shown.

<Note>
  Times you type into a correction are wall-clock times in your practice's time zone: the same clock the session record itself uses. See [Which clock is which](/scheduling/which-clock).
</Note>

## Who can sign, and who can correct

You write and sign your own clinical records. Other counselors on the case can read them; they don't write in them, and neither do practice administrators.

The one exception exists so a record can't be orphaned: if the counselor who wrote a session has left the practice, a correction can still be filed against it. Nobody's departure freezes a mistake in place permanently.

## Why not just allow editing?

Because the moment a signed record can be edited, every signed record becomes a record of what someone was willing to say *today* about a session months ago. Corrections that sit visibly beside the original cost you one extra click and keep the account honest: for your counselee, for your supervisor, and for you if anyone ever asks what happened.

## Archiving is not deleting

A locked session can be **archived**, which takes it out of your working lists. It isn't gone: the button becomes **Unarchive**, and the record is still there and still readable. Nothing about signing, locking or addenda changes.
