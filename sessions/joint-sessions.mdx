---
title: "Joint sessions"
description: "Couples and families on one case, with each person's record kept their own."
---

A couple or a family is counseled on **one shared case**: one course of care, with more than one counselee on its roster. Every session on that case is a joint session.

## Setting one up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start the case">
    Start a case from one person's record.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the others">
    On the case page, under **Roster**, click **Add counselee** for each person. A case with two people reads as a **couple** case, with more as a **family** case.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Book on the shared case">
    When you book an appointment, choose the shared case (for example *Marcus Pérez & Wanda Pérez · couple*). Everyone on the roster is an attendee.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If someone stops coming, click **End** beside their name on the roster. The roster is kept as history, so you can always see who was on the case and when.

## During the session

The session page has one **Shared note**, for what was said in front of everyone, and then **a record for each counselee**: their own check-in score, their own narrative and their own homework.

## Each person's record is their own

Being on the same case doesn't make two people's records one record.

- Each counselee's own narrative, check-ins and homework belong to them.
- In the portal, a counselee sees only what's theirs: their own homework, their own documents, their own messages.
- Intake forms, safety notes and files stay with the person they're about.

Write things that concern one person in their own record, not in the shared note.

## Session numbers

A session where everyone on the case was there gets the next whole session number; one where someone was missing gets a part number (such as 2.1). To decide yourself, open the appointment and set **Counts as a full session** to **Yes** or **No**.

## Two counselors

A joint case can have a co-counselor as well as a primary. A co-counselor can read the case's sessions and notes; each counselor writes only their own. See [Assigning counselors](/counselees/assigning-counselors).
